# Candidate comparison — The Houseplant Out-of-Office

Judge brief (most important first): (1) reads as an out-of-office auto-reply
a houseplant would send; (2) actually funny — a fresh joke that surprises,
not a mild pun or stock office phrase; (3) the plant's own life (water,
roots, sunlight, long stretches of being ignored) drives the joke; (4) tight
inside 120 characters, no wasted words; (5) original — no office cliches.

## The 8 candidates

| # | Chars | Line |
|---|-------|------|
| 1 | 114 | Thank you for your email. I'm a fern. I died in March. This auto-reply is the only thing on this desk still alive. |
| 2 | 114 | Out of office: I'm a cactus. Your email is filed under 'things I'll handle when it rains.' It never rains in here. |
| 3 | 111 | I'm out of office, photosynthesizing. I'll reply at the speed of growth, which is to say never. Water me first. |
| 4 | 115 | Hi, I'm the office monstera. I'm out of office until someone moves me closer to the window. That someone is not me. |
| 5 | 117 | Out of office: I've been watering myself with hope since March. If I wilt, tell my owner Wi-Fi password died with me. |
| 6 | 118 | This is an automated reply from the fern on your desk. I haven't been watered since March, and neither has your inbox. |
| 7 | 109 | I'm a houseplant and I'm out of office. I'll get back to you as soon as someone remembers I exist. So: never. |
| 8 | 120 | Out of office: I'm busy turning sunlight into resentment. Urgent matters go to the spider plant. It doesn't care either. |

## Verdict

**Chosen: #1 (114 chars).** It is the only candidate where every criterion
fires at once: it opens exactly like a real auto-reply; the surprise is a
genuine twist (the fern is already dead — the reply outlived the plant); the
plant's life, neglect since March, is the joke rather than decoration; and
the punchline lands in the final words with nothing wasted.

- #2 is the runner-up: the cactus voice is strong and "it never rains in
  here" is a real punchline, but the filing-cabinet setup is a step more
  office-generic than #1's dead-fern twist.
- #8 has the best single image ("turning sunlight into resentment") but its
  second sentence deflates rather than escalates.
- #6 has a neat parallel ("neither has your inbox") but no surprise.
- #3, #4 and #7 land their jokes early and then explain them; #5 spends its
  best line ("watering myself with hope") on setup and ends on a Wi-Fi gag
  that drifts off the plant.
